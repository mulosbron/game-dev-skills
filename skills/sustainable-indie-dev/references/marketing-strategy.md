# Marketing & Strategy

## Never Market on Hope

"If the game is good, it'll sell" is not a strategy. Build a data-driven plan from day one.

## Trinity Hook Principle

Every marketable game needs three strong hooks:

1. **Story hook** — A narrative premise that grabs attention in one sentence
2. **Mechanic hook** — Gameplay that's genuinely unique or combines genres in a new way
3. **Visual hook** — An art style that stops someone mid-scroll

Missing any one severely limits discoverability. Audit all three before investing in production.

## Early Steam Page

Set up the Steam page (or equivalent platform presence) long before the game is finished. Wishlists accumulate slowly — months of lead time are normal.

## Build Your Own Audience

A publisher deal or platform algorithm is not a safety net. Build direct relationships:
- 10,000 loyal followers is an insurance policy for an indie dev
- Social media presence (Twitter/X, TikTok, YouTube, Discord)
- Email list for direct communication
- Regular devlogs showing progress and personality

## The Polished Demo

After the prototype, produce a 15-minute fully polished demo. This is the business card:
- Players get a real taste and give feedback
- Investors and publishers see execution quality
- Press and content creators have something concrete to cover

"Polished" means no placeholder art, no broken controls, no tutorial gaps.

## Content Strategy

Share the development journey, not just the final product:
- Devlogs with progress and lessons learned
- Behind-the-scenes looks at systems and decisions
- GIFs and short clips of mechanics in action
- People follow the developer's story as much as the game itself
