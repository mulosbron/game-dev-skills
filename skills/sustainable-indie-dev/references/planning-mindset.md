# Planning & Mindset

## Realistic Scoping

Starting with the dream game maximizes the chance of abandonment. Pick small, finishable projects instead.

**Time budget exercise:** Ask "How many years can I sustain this project?" Halve the answer — that's the real timeline. Things always take at least 2x longer than estimated.

## Build Systems, Not Games

Work on individual, reusable systems: inventory, AI, dialogue, movement. Games change direction; systems persist across projects.

## Play to Strengths

Decide based on existing skills, not aspirations. Can't model 3D? Choose pixel-art or isometric. Route around weaknesses instead of fighting them.

## Modularity

Pre-made parts speed up development and reduce spaghetti code. Build components that plug into future projects.

## Pivot Without Guilt

Ideas on paper rarely survive contact with implementation. If an "island system" feels boring, reshape it into an "open world system." Flexibility beats attachment.

## Anti-Patterns

- Starting with a 5-year RPG as a solo dev's first project
- Estimating "6 months" for a game with no prototype
- Refusing to cut features because "they're important" without testing them first
- Building everything from scratch when assets and tools exist
