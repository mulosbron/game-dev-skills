# Psychology & Motivation

## Motivation vs Discipline

Motivation fluctuates — some days you won't want to work at all. Discipline is what carries the project forward on those days. One line of code or one sprite on a bad day still counts.

## Burnout Is Normal

Stepping away from the project is productive, not lazy. Returning with a fresh mind solves problems faster than pushing through exhaustion. Schedule breaks before they're forced.

## Community as Fuel

Share work publicly. Even a single "looks cool" comment can sustain motivation through a tough week. Criticism provides direction when feeling lost.

## Small Wins

Don't focus on "finishing the game" as the only win condition. Celebrate micro-milestones:
- "Today I finished the inventory system"
- "This week I got the combat loop feeling good"
- "This month I shipped a playable prototype"

Progress feeling is what keeps the marathon going.

## Don't Work Alone (If Possible)

Even one collaborator changes the dynamic:
- An artist makes the game look real sooner
- A musician makes it feel alive
- An accountability partner keeps the pace up

Solo dev is viable but harder. Seek support wherever possible.

## Anti-Patterns

- Working 12-hour days for weeks, then crashing for a month
- Ignoring physical health (sleep, exercise, posture)
- Comparing progress to full-time studios or successful indie launches
- Treating the project as a sprint when it's a marathon
- Isolating completely — no community, no peers, no feedback
