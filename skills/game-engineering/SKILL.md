# Game Engineering

Mechanics are the plate, fork and knife; the meal is what the player feels. Adding points, badges and leaderboards is not design. First name the emotion you are aiming for, then derive the mechanic from it.

## Diagnostic order (for a boring game)
1. **30-second loop:** action → immediate feedback → reward → repeat. Which link in the chain is broken?
2. **Flow:** does difficulty match the player's skill? Too easy bores, too hard drives away. Raise difficulty together with skill.
3. **Octalysis:** how many of the eight drives are active? Most dead games lean only on "accomplishment" (2). Pick the missing one: meaning (1), creativity (3), ownership (4), social (5), scarcity (6), curiosity (7), loss avoidance (8). Keep white hat (1,3,5) and black hat (6,7,8) in balance; black hat alone leads to burnout.
4. **Player type:** achiever (progression), explorer (hidden content, multiple paths), socializer (teams, status), competitor (rankings). Designing only for achievers loses most of the room. Not all four are mandatory; decide which one is the target.

## Reward design
- Durability order: **Status > Access > Power > Stuff**. A status reward must require the behavior it rewards.
- Never reward a behavior that is already intrinsically motivated; it kills the motivation (overjustification).
- Don't reward every behavior; inflation zeroes the reward. Don't hand a reward over casually; casual delivery devalues it.
- Delivery kinds: fixed, random (wheel), surprise, long-term raffle, must-be-shared, set completion.

## Player journey
Onboarding (individual, easy, personal goal) → Scaffolding (social comparison, goal adjusts to skill) → Endgame (contribution, mentoring, advanced challenge). Don't throw the player straight into competition; don't leave the endgame empty.

## Juicy feedback
Immediate, inviting, repeatable, coherent with the fiction, continuous, non-interrupting, balanced, occasionally unexpected. Instead of "you failed": "your first attempt just made history, ready to beat your record?".
